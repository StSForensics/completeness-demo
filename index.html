Integrity is not completeness

Integrity: nothing in the records I was given has been changed. Completeness: nothing that should be there is missing, for the scope the operator declared. Pick a scenario. What this is, for your role

Anyone: A diary can have no page altered and still be missing the page that matters. This demo shows a log that passes a tamper check while entries are missing, and what extra evidence catches it.

Business and risk owners: A clean integrity report says the records you were handed are unchanged. It says nothing about whether they are all there. Ask who holds an independent count of the entries, and which outside system (payment provider, storage audit) can be reconciled against the log.

Insurers, reinsurers and insurance data standards: Think of a claims listing or bordereau that adds up perfectly but leaves out a claim. Self-consistency is a hygiene check. Coverage needs a declared scope and a reconciliation against a record the preparer does not control.

AI and ML engineers: Each entry hashes the previous hash plus its own body. The integrity check (every entry matches its own fingerprint) catches edits. The gap check (numbers and links run unbroken) catches a deletion in the middle. Anyone can recompute a chain, so a cut tail or a fully rebuilt log still verifies unless a third party holds a checkpoint (entry count and head hash). An agent action that never wrote a log line shows up only against a system the agent does not control.

IETF and standards people: The toy has no signatures, no Merkle tree and no timestamps. The checkpoint stands in for a signed tree head. Real logs add signed heads and consistency proofs so a witness can check that a log only grew (RFC 9162, Certificate Transparency 2.0). RFC 9943 (SCITT) gives receipts showing a statement was registered. Whether every relevant action was submitted is a scope question each deployment still has to answer. The draft O4M Record Tests (C1 to C23, comments open until 1 December 2026) focus on that omission question and are meant to sit beside formats such as SCITT.

Where this sits: VerZan Check asks "is this exactly the file someone recorded?" This page asks the next question: is anything missing from the record?

How to read the results

Integrity: is each entry still exactly as it was fingerprinted?

Completeness, three tests in order: (a) no gap inside the log, (b) the log agrees with a witness checkpoint (entry count and head hash), (c) the log agrees with records held by outside parties.

Integrity shows PASS or FAIL. Completeness shows FAIL, HOLD (the evidence supplied cannot settle it) or NO GAP FOUND. That is the best it can say, and it is never proof that nothing is missing.

Limits of this toy

One writer, one witness, no signatures. The witness is assumed honest and independent of the operator; if the operator controls the checkpoint they can rewrite it as well. Completeness is always relative to a scope the operator declared. NO GAP FOUND means the three tests agreed. It does not mean nothing is missing. Outside records are matched by exact text, and only for the actions that outside party can see.
